Stress and accents

Without an accent mark, words ending in a vowel, n or s usually stress the penultimate syllable (e.g. CA-sa). Other endings normally stress the last syllable (pa-PEL). Written accents can override this rule (e.g. MÚ-si-ca).

Regional differences: c before e/i and z usually sound /s/ in Latin America, but /θ/ in much of Spain. The sounds written ll and consonantal y are merged for many speakers (yeísmo). These are broad teaching descriptions, not a single universal accent.
